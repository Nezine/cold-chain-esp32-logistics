# Lead Exercises

Your job is to keep the team focused and make sure everyone knows what to do next. You do not need to write all the code.

## Exercise 1: Agree on the Project

1. Write one simple sentence: “Our system helps ___ keep ___ at the right temperature while it is being delivered.”
2. Write down who will use it, such as a dispatcher, warehouse worker, or manager.
3. Write down what you will not build this week, such as payments, maps, or user accounts.
4. Save the agreed list in `shared/requirements/`.

**Done when:** all five members describe the project in the same way.

## Exercise 2: Make a Team Schedule

1. Give each major task one owner.
2. Write what each person will hand to the next person. Example: “The sensor member gives the backend member sample temperature readings.”
3. Choose a short meeting time each day.
4. Write down the schedule in `shared/meeting-notes/`.

**Done when:** every member knows their task, deadline, and who reviews their work.

## Exercise 3: Agree on Important Words

Ask the group to explain these words in your own project:

- Shipment: the goods being delivered.
- Temperature reading: a temperature sent by a sensor.
- Alert: a warning that something needs attention.
- Dispatch response: what the delivery team does after an alert.

1. Add any other important words.
2. When the group disagrees, write down the question.
3. Discuss it and record the final answer and reason in `shared/meeting-notes/`.

**Done when:** nobody uses the same word to mean different things.

## Exercise 4: Check the Main Demonstration

Use this simple story:

1. A user creates a shipment.
2. The sensor sends a temperature.
3. The system notices a temperature problem.
4. The user sees an alert.
5. The user records a response.

Ask each member what they will provide for each step. Write down missing pieces and risks in `shared/reviews/`.

**Done when:** the team can explain the full story from beginning to end.

## Exercise 5: Prepare the Final Review

1. Read each requirement and ask, “Can we show this working?”
2. Check that the alert can be followed from the sensor reading to the user response.
3. Ask each member to explain their work in two minutes.
4. Write the demonstration order and remaining limitations.

**Done when:** the team can give the demonstration without guessing what comes next.
